## §4 — Blind Spot #1: Secured vWAN hubs have stateful firewalls

**Teaches:** The article's "typically no firewalls" assumption is false in secured vWAN. Asymmetric routing no longer "just degrades performance" — it silently kills sessions.

The article says: *"Typically, over the ExpressRoute private peering path you don't come across stateful entities such as NAT or Firewalls. Therefore, asymmetrical routing over ExpressRoute private peering doesn't necessarily block traffic flow."* In secured vWAN with routing intent private, EVERY on-prem-bound packet passes through the hub's AzFW. Asymmetric routing means the forward SYN hits AzFW1 (creates flow state); the return SYN-ACK hits AzFW2 (no state → TCP state-engine drop). Azure Firewall does not log TCP stateful drops. The failure is invisible in the policy layer — it looks like the on-prem host is not responding.

## §5 — Blind Spot #2: Partner-managed CEs own the AS path between carrier and on-prem

**Teaches:** The article's "use iBGP local-pref on the on-prem CE" recommendation is inapplicable in carrier/cloud-interconnect scenarios.

The article: *"you can use local preference on the iBGP session between your BGP routers (on which ExpressRoute circuits are terminated on on-premises side)"*. In partner ER scenarios — Megaport MCR, GCP Cloud Interconnect, AWS Direct Connect, Equinix Fabric — the CE router is the carrier's virtual switch, not a box the customer owns. There is no iBGP session the customer can set local-pref on. The GCP Cloud Router (ASN 16550, our on-prem-side proxy) exposes only `CUSTOM` vs `DEFAULT` advertise_mode — no per-peer outbound route policy. Design C makes this visible: one CR with two peers, BGP best-path selection is pure AS-path length (Mech B Axis-1 prepend), not local-pref. The customer's levers are: vWAN Route Maps on the Azure side, and carrier-managed prefix filters (if the carrier provides them).

## §6 — Blind Spot #3: vWAN Route Maps are the modern AS-path lever

**Teaches:** The article's AS-path prepend at the on-prem CE has a vWAN-native equivalent — Route Maps applied at the Azure vHub, no CE change required.

vWAN Route Maps (post-dating the article) let you prepend ASNs on inbound ER advertisements at the vHub. Two implementations map directly to the article's scenarios — covered in sequence:

## §7 — Bonus: The reserved-ASN prepend trick (Mech C1 deep-dive)

**Teaches:** Prepending a reserved ASN at the Azure layer is diagnostically cleaner than prepending your own ASN at the CE.

In Mech C1, the Route Map prepends a reserved ASN (e.g., AS_TRANS 23456 or an RFC 5398 documentation ASN) rather than the customer's own ASN. This is diagnostically cleaner: anyone reading `gcloud compute routers get-status` immediately recognises the reserved ASN as synthetic prepend, not a real AS in the path. It also avoids polluting AS-path analytics with the customer's ASN and works in partner scenarios where the customer doesn't control the on-prem CE. vWAN Route Maps strip the reserved ASN from advertisements propagated further — the manipulation stays local to the hub.

## §9 — What the article still gets right

**Teaches:** End with humility. This is an update, not a correction.

- **More-specific route** advertisement still works in vWAN with or without route maps — unchanged.
- **Connection weight** still works for Azure→on-prem path selection, but does NOT fix the stateful AzFW drop — that requires route maps or AS-path prepend on the return path.
- **BFD** recommendation: still correct, more important than ever with AzFW in the path.
- **ECMP behavior**: 4-circuit max, still applies under vWAN.
- **Scenario 1 and 2 architectures** remain valid — this blog adds the vWAN-native recipe.
